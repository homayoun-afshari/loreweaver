# The Ice Citadel

## Overview

The Ice Citadel stands on the Northern Reach, on a spur of black rock above a frozen sea. Its walls are cut from blue glacial ice and set on foundations of dark stone. About eight hundred people live there, most of them Frost Wardens, their families and the servants who keep the Citadel running. It is the seat of the Frost Wardens and the coldest inhabited place in Aethel.

The Citadel is reached by the Gate of Frost in the western wall. The winter lasts nine months, and the Long Winter, the hardest forty days of the year, closes every road to the Citadel except the Ash Road to the south, which is usually passable.

## Layout

### The four walls

The Citadel has four walls: north, south, east and west. All four are warded. A warded wall cannot be crossed by teleportation, in either direction, so nobody can teleport into the Citadel or out of it. The western wall holds the Gate of Frost, which is the main entrance. The eastern wall holds a small door called the Eastern Postern, which is kept barred and guarded all year.

### The Hall of Embers

At the heart of the Citadel is the Hall of Embers, where the Great Hearth burns day and night. The Great Hearth is an ordinary fire, fed with peat and driftwood. Fire magic is smothered inside the Citadel, but ordinary fire is not magic, so the Hall of Embers is the warmest room in the fortress and the place where the Wardens eat, argue and keep their records.

### The Flint Vault

Below the Hall of Embers is the Flint Vault, a cold cellar where the Wardens store their flint. Flint is rare in the north. The Wardens hand it out only to people they trust. A visitor can be lent a flint shard at the Gate of Frost, after signing the Ledger of Visitors, and must return the shard when leaving. Keeping a flint without permission is theft, and the punishment is a year of service in the Vault.

## The Wards

### How the wards work

The four walls of the Citadel are protected by wards that were raised after the founding of the fortress. They stop teleportation and scrying. Only the Warden-General can lower a ward, with the seal of the Frost Wardens, and only for one hour at a time.

### The Night of Thaw

Once a year, on the last night of the Long Winter, the eastern wall of the Citadel is unwarded. The ward on the eastern wall goes down at midnight and comes back up at dawn. The Wardens say that the ice must be allowed to breathe while the Frost Binding renews itself.

During the Night of Thaw the eastern wall can be crossed by teleportation like any open ground, and the guard on the Eastern Postern is doubled. The other three walls stay warded. The Frost Binding that smothers fire magic stays in force all night, so fire magic is still impossible inside the Citadel on the Night of Thaw unless the caster possesses flint.

## The Visitor's Primer

The Wardens give every visitor a short booklet called the Visitor's Primer. It lists the rules of the Citadel in plain words. It says that a visitor who wishes to cast fire magic must strike their flint on the steel before every casting, and that an unstruck flint is not enough.

This instruction does not agree with the law of magic recorded in the Archive of the Glass Library, which says that carrying the flint is enough. Visitors who have followed the Primer and struck their flint have never been refused, so nobody has tested which rule is right.

## Travel

The Ash Road runs south from the Citadel to Ember Keep. Travellers on foot need nine days to cover it. A rider on a fast horse can do it in five days. The road is open all year, except during the Long Winter, when drifts can close it for a week at a time.
