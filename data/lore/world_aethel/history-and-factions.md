# History and Factions of Aethel

The history of Aethel is counted in years from the Sundering. Year 0 is the year of the Sundering, and every date in this document is counted from it.

## Timeline

| Year    | Event                                              |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 0       | The Sundering                                      |
| 31      | The Ice Citadel is founded                         |
| 212-219 | The War of Embers                                  |

## The Sundering

The Sundering was the catastrophe that broke the old world. In a single night the sky split, the central plains were fused into glass, and mana rose close to the surface of the land, where it had never been before. Before the Sundering, magic was a craft for a few scholars. After it, anyone born with a pool of mana could cast.

The Sundering also changed the price of magic. Teleportation, which had cost 30 mana before the Sundering, cost 50 mana after it, because the same journey now had to cross land that the Sundering had made unstable. The fields of fused glass are still there, and the Glass Library was built on them.

## The Founding of the Ice Citadel

In Year 31 the Warden-General Isolde Frane led a band of survivors north to the Northern Reach and began to build the Ice Citadel. She chose the site because the ice there was the oldest in the world and the cold slowed the wild mana that the Sundering had released. The Frost Binding, the working that smothers fire magic inside the walls, was her first great spell. She had learned it from a flint-bearer, a person who carried a flint as a token of an older craft, and she made flint the single exception to the Binding in thanks.

## The War of Embers

The War of Embers lasted from Year 212 to Year 219. It was the only great war between the Frost Wardens and the Ember Court, and it began over iron.

### The causes

For two hundred years the Frost Wardens had bought ember iron from the forges of Ember Keep. In Year 212 the Ember Court doubled the price, saying that the Wardens had grown rich on the trade in northern furs. The Wardens refused to pay, and when the Court stopped selling them iron the Wardens began to buy it from the Salt Marshes instead. The Court called this a betrayal, and the first soldiers marched north in the spring of that year.

### The siege

The decisive fight of the war was the Siege of the Cinder Gate in Year 215. The Wardens brought frost magic to Ember Keep and attacked the Cinder Gate for forty days. Inside the Keep every frost spell cost double mana, so the Wardens burned through their pools in a few days and had to rest on the plain in the heat. Many of them were captured. The Cinder Guard, whose captains were mostly mana-blind, were not affected by the Wardens' charms, and held the gate. The Siege is remembered in the Keep as the Day the Frost Failed.

### The end of the war

After the siege the Wardens could not take the Keep, and the Court could not reach the Citadel, whose walls were warded against teleportation. Both sides were exhausted. The war ended in Year 219 with the Concord of Glass.

## The Concord of Glass

The Concord of Glass was signed in the Glass Library in Year 219 by the Warden-General, the Ember Regent and the Head Archivist. It has four terms. First, blood-casting is forbidden, and the Archivists have the right to inspect any caster suspected of it. Second, the Glass Library is neutral ground, and no weapon may be drawn inside it. Third, the wards of the Ice Citadel are recognised as the Wardens' own affair. Fourth, Ember Keep may stay unwarded, as the Ember Court wishes, and no ward may be raised around it without the Court's consent.

## The First Night of Thaw

In Year 220, a year after the Concord, a Warden discovered that the eastern ward of the Citadel went down at midnight on the last night of the Long Winter and came up again at dawn. The Wardens named it the Night of Thaw. They did not announce it, and for a time the knowledge was kept inside the Citadel.

The official chronicle of the Wardens states that the wards of the Citadel have never failed. The records of the Glass Library disagree. They say that the eastern ward has gone down on the Night of Thaw every year since Year 220. The Wardens answer that a ward that goes down on a schedule has not failed, and that the ward is only resting. Whether the Night of Thaw is a flaw or a design has never been settled.

## Factions

### The Frost Wardens

The Frost Wardens are the order that rules the Ice Citadel and the Northern Reach. They are led by the Warden-General, who holds the seal of the Citadel. They value discipline, patience and the old ways, and they distrust the Ember Court. The Wardens train flint-bearers, scouts who are licensed to carry flint out of the Flint Vault.

### The Ember Court

The Ember Court rules Ember Keep and the Cinderlands. It is led by the Ember Regent and a council of master smiths. The Court values strength, trade and independence, and it has never allowed a ward to be raised around the Keep. Its soldiers are the Cinder Guard.

### The Archivists of Glass

The Archivists of Glass keep the Glass Library and the records of the world. They are neutral between the Wardens and the Court. They are led by the Head Archivist, assisted by the Deputy Archivist and the Keeper of the Concord. They award mana ranks and inspect casters suspected of blood-casting.

## Distances

The Wardens' chronicle says that the march from Ember Keep to the Ice Citadel takes a fortnight, because an army moves slowly and must carry its supplies. The Archive of the Glass Library says that a traveller on foot needs nine days, and a rider five. The figures describe different kinds of travel, but the chronicle does not say so.
